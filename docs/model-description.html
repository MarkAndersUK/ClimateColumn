Model reference

A one-dimensional radiative‑convective atmospheric column

13,507 lines of C# that march a 50 km column of air to radiative–convective equilibrium, with longwave opacity built from real HITRAN line data rather than a tuned grey constant.

What it computes

Given a solar input, a planetary albedo and an absorber loading, it solves for the temperature at every level of an atmospheric column and at the surface beneath it — the state in which the column radiates to space exactly what it absorbs, and no level is left with a net heating tendency.

Vertical structure

Eighty segments of 625 m each, from the surface to 50 km. Pressure and the initial temperature profile come from the U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976; each segment’s air mass is its pressure drop divided by gravity, which fixes its heat capacity and its share of the absorber.

Longwave transfer

Emission follows the Koenigsberger volumetric law — a slab of thickness dz emits 4ε′σT⁴dz per unit horizontal area, split equally between the two hemispheres. Kirchhoff’s law then sets the hemispheric absorptivity equal to the emissivity, which pins the extinction coefficient in flux space at Dε′.

dF±/dz = ∓ D ε′ ( F± − σT⁴ ) Integrated across a constant-temperature segment this gives an exponential recurrence that is exact at any optical thickness — subdividing an isothermal slab leaves its emission unchanged.

The diffusivity factor is D = 2, and that is a derivation rather than a fit. The exact hemispheric transmission is 2E₃(τ), which expands as 1 − 2τ for small τ; matching the volumetric emission law in its own optically thin limit therefore forces D = 2. The commonly used 1.66 is an empirical compromise for thick paths, and is available for comparison runs.

Spectral treatment

A grey column gets the greenhouse effect roughly right and the CO₂ response badly wrong, so opacity is built from measured line data instead. Line lists for six molecules — H₂O (rotational and bending), CO₂, O₃, CH₄, N₂O — are fetched from HITRAN, laid on a shared wavenumber grid, and the band boundaries are then derived from where those lines actually sit rather than chosen by hand.

The derivation recovers the atmospheric window and ozone’s position inside it without being told either exists. Six molecules in sixteen bands cover 98.9 % of a 260 K Planck function, leaving a 1.1 % remainder.

Correlated-k

Within each band, absorption varies by orders of magnitude between line cores and wings, so a single mean coefficient is useless. The resolved spectrum is sorted into a cumulative distribution and integrated with a quadrature over that distribution:

T(u) = ∫₀¹ e−k(g)u dg ≈ Σⱼ wⱼ e−kⱼu Gauss–Hermite nodes in log k, which at 16 points is about 200× more accurate than equal-probability midpoints. Sixteen g-points per band are used by default, with lines evaluated out to 400 cm⁻¹ from centre — see the note on the logarithm for why that cutoff is the parameter that matters.

A line-by-line calculation is kept as the reference the band approximations are judged against. It is what showed that grey bands are not merely imprecise but catastrophically wrong: at τ = 10 a grey band transmits 5×10⁻⁵ where real water vapour transmits 0.504.

Why the CO₂ response is logarithmic

Doubling CO₂ adds about the same forcing whatever the starting concentration — 285 → 570 ppm gives roughly what 570 → 1140 gives. This is not a thermodynamic law and it is not an insulation-thickness effect. It is a property of the shape of the absorption spectrum, and it comes out in three steps.

1 · The band centre is already saturated

At present-day concentrations the core of the 15 µm CO₂ band is completely opaque. Radiation there already escapes from high in the atmosphere, and adding CO₂ changes nothing: an opaque band cannot get more opaque. So the forcing cannot come from the centre.

2 · It comes from the wings, and the wing shape sets the law

What adding CO₂ does is widen the opaque interval outward into the flanks, where absorption is still weak. The forcing is roughly the width of that interval times the Planck difference between the surface and the level the band now emits from. So the law follows directly from how absorption falls off with distance from the band centre.

If absorption decays exponentially away from the centre, k(ν) = k₀ e−|Δν|/a, the band is opaque wherever k(ν)·u > 1, i.e. wherever

|Δν| < a · ln(k₀u) ⟹ W(u) = 2a · ln(k₀u) The opaque width grows as the logarithm of the absorber amount, so F ≈ W·ΔB gives dF/d(ln u) = constant. Each doubling widens the opaque interval by the same increment — which is exactly what "logarithmic forcing" means.

3 · The wing shape is a fact about CO₂, not a general rule

This is where the explanation earns its keep, because other wing shapes give other laws. A single isolated pressure-broadened line has a Lorentz far wing, k ∝ 1/Δν²; that gives an opaque width growing as √u, so an isolated line would produce a square-root law, not a logarithm. Purely Gaussian flanks would give √(ln u).

So the logarithm is not automatic. It holds because the 15 µm band’s collective envelope — many overlapping rotational lines, their strengths set by the Boltzmann distribution over rotational states — happens to decay close to exponentially across the range that matters. That is an empirical property of the measured spectrum; it is the subject of current literature rather than something derivable from a one-line argument, and it is the reason the logarithm eventually fails at concentrations far outside the fitted range.

What this model demonstrates

The claim above is checkable here, and it checks out from both directions. A grey absorber has no spectral structure at all, and its forcing ratio against the accepted law grows from 1.17 to 2.14 across 285–1000 ppm — the wrong shape, not merely the wrong size. Replace it with the sixteen HITRAN-derived bands and the response straightens out: fitting F = A ln(C/C₀) gives A between 6.84 and 7.10 W m⁻² per ln, a 3.6 % drift across a 3.5× range.

The logarithm arrives with the spectroscopy and cannot be produced without it. What remains wrong is a near-uniform scale factor of about 1.3, which is a far more tractable failure than one that changes shape with concentration.

The resolution had to be earned, not assumed. An earlier version of this configuration used 8 bands and a 15 cm⁻¹ wing cutoff and reported almost exactly the same coefficient — but by error cancellation. Widening the cutoff alone took it to 9.35, badly wrong, because truncated wings and a coarse band split had been compensating. Since the logarithm comes from the far wings, throwing them away is the one approximation this figure cannot afford. The converged settings are 16 bands, 16 g-points and a 400 cm⁻¹ cutoff; the published ratio moved from 1.32 to 1.33.

The chart the test suite generates compares the two forcings directly in W/m² — the model’s own instantaneous forcing against the reference equilibrium, beside 5.35 ln(C/C₀). At 1000 ppm that is 8.909 against 6.716. Nothing is converted, so no climate sensitivity is borrowed; an earlier version plotted temperature instead, which required the model’s own sensitivity and thereby made the reference partly a restatement of the thing it was testing.

Convection and the surface

Three non-radiative terms. A bulk sensible heat flux h_c(T_s − T_air) with h_c = 5.8 + 4.1v; an optional latent flux; and a critical-lapse-rate adjustment that mixes any superadiabatic block onto exactly 6.5 K/km while conserving Σ CᵢTᵢ.

The surface is deliberately excluded from the adjustment — it exchanges heat with the air only through radiation and the turbulent fluxes, and mixing the surface reservoir too would double-count that transfer and leave its energy budget open.

Evaporation is off by default. It is the largest non-radiative term in a real surface budget (~80 W/m² against ~20 for sensible heat), but h_c here was calibrated with the sensible flux standing in for both, so switching it on is a different model rather than a repair to this one. Turned on, it reproduces Earth’s partition — the Bowen ratio hits 0.26 near moisture availability 0.35 — but not the magnitude: the total turbulent flux tops out near 41 W/m² against Earth’s ~100, because h_c is a building-physics film coefficient rather than a global-mean bulk transfer coefficient.

Sphericity turns on one identity: writing the two-stream equation in terms of G = (r/r₀)²F removes the geometric term entirely and leaves the plane-parallel equation with its source scaled by (r/r₀)² — so the exact recurrence survives and sphericity enters in one line.

The solar cross-section is the one that changes the answer, and the obvious implementation of it is wrong twice: the extra light is limb light, grazing through the atmosphere and out the other side, so it never touches the surface and the albedo has nothing to act through. Done properly as a slant-path integral over impact parameter, 2.563 of the 5.362 W/m² intercepted is absorbed — a third less than rescaling by the area ratio would give — and it lands at 19.2 km rather than at the ground.

What it is not

  • No clouds and no scattering. Clear-sky longwave only; the Schwarzschild equation solved has absorption and emission terms and nothing else.
  • One angle. The exact hemispheric integral 2π∫I(z,μ)μ dμ is collapsed onto a single diffusivity, and 2E₃(τ) appears only in the tests, never in the solver. D = 2 is exact in the optically thin limit, which makes it the worst choice for opaque band centres — no single value can serve bands spanning τ ≪ 1 to τ ≫ 1.
  • Line strengths are not temperature-scaled. Half-widths are — HITRAN’s n_air is applied — but intensities are used at their 296 K values, since scaling them needs total internal partition sums. Voigt profiles, line mixing and sub-Lorentzian far wings are absent.
  • Shortwave is not radiative transfer. A prescribed fraction split by air mass and a Chapman profile, with no zenith angle and no diurnal cycle.
  • No prognostic humidity. Water vapour enters as an absorber scaled by Clausius–Clapeyron at fixed relative humidity, so the convective adjustment conserves dry enthalpy rather than moist static energy.
  • One column. No horizontal transport, no ice–albedo feedback, and the absorber amounts are illustrative rather than fitted to observations. Read the CO₂ results as spectroscopy, not as a prediction.

How it is checked

323 tests. The ones that carry weight are not the unit tests but the independent references: closed-form analytic solutions the model must reproduce (grey radiative equilibrium, the (N+1)1/4 opaque-slab stack), a line-by-line spectrum the band approximations are measured against, exact numerical integrals for the exponential integral and the Planck fraction, and — for the spherical solver — a fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration of the original spherical equation, sharing nothing with the solver but its inputs.

Several are mutation-checked: inverting the sign of the shell-area factor fails five tests, and pairing the geopotential grid with a constant gravity fails three. Energy closure is asserted everywhere — what the surface and every segment absorb must sum to what the planet is said to absorb.

HITRAN data is not committed, so the suite builds and runs offline with no network and no external files; the tests that need real lines report themselves as not-run rather than failing.
